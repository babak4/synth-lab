## What it is

Your computer keyboard becomes a piano. The bottom two rows (Z to the slash key, with S, D, G, H, J, L and semicolon for the black keys) are the lower keyboard. The top two rows (Q to the right bracket, with the number keys for black keys) are the upper keyboard.

## What you'll hear

The keys play the notes in the picture of the piano below. Each key is read by its position, so the layout works the same on QWERTY, AZERTY and QWERTZ keyboards. The left and right arrows change octave, and the up and down arrows change [velocity](term:velocity). Hold Shift as a sustain pedal. Press Escape to stop everything.

## Try this

Each experiment changes the sound a little so that you can hear how playing affects it.

## The controls

- [Octave](help:kbd-octave): shift both rows by whole octaves.
- [Velocity](help:kbd-velocity): how hard the notes are played.

## Beyond this synth

Laptop keys can only tell you whether a key is down. A real **MIDI** keyboard also senses how hard you play, and many have **aftertouch**, which measures pressure after the key is down. Other controllers include pads, wind controllers, and **MPE** surfaces such as the Seaboard and the Osmose, where moving a finger on a key bends the note.

Many synths also respond to mod and pitch wheels and to per-note expression.

A laptop can only register a few keys at once. This is [key ghosting](term:ghosting), and it means some chords will not sound.

## Where you've heard it

Every keyboard player uses an octave button or a transposer. The sustain pedal is what makes piano music flow.
